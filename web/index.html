How it works

Show. Confirm. Speak.

Three steps, and the signer stays in control: SignMate never speaks a word that nobody confirmed.

  1. 1

    Show the sign

    Sign one word in front of the camera. MediaPipe finds 21 points on each hand in every frame, on your device. When your hands come down, SignMate knows the sign has ended.

  2. 2

    Confirm the word

    SignMate suggests a word in English and Urdu. If it is not sure, it shows its top three and asks “Which one did you sign?”. If it cannot tell, it says so. Nothing is spoken until you tap Yes.

  3. 3

    Speak, and get a reply

    SignMate says the word aloud in Urdu, Roman Urdu or English, and always shows it as text too. Words build into a sentence. The hearing person types a reply, which appears in large type, with real PSL videos for the words SignMate knows.

These screens come from SignMate's guided tour, which replays a real recorded sign from the SignSpeak dataset (hand points only, no video) so it works without a camera. The answer on screen is the shipped model's saved answer for that recording.

About the camera

  • Your browser will ask to use the camera. Choose Allow; SignMate cannot work without it.
  • The video stays on this device. SignMate turns each frame into hand points in memory and never uploads, records or stores the picture. There is no server that could receive it.
  • You can stop the camera at any time with the Stop button, or by closing the tab.
  • If you would rather not use a camera, the guided tour replays recorded signs instead.

Tips for a good result

  • Face a light source, with both hands and your shoulders in view, about an arm's length or more from the camera.
  • Start with your hands down. Raise them, make one sign, then lower them again.
  • Good signs to start with: Assalam-o-alaikum, Hello, Thank you, Goodbye, Water, Help. The sign library lists all .

Sign library

All signs SignMate knows

This list is read from the model's own labels file (models/psl/manifest.json) when the site is built, so it is exactly what the camera can recognise. are one-handed and two-handed. Every sign is “Suggested, please confirm” (Tier B). The model also has a 70th class, “Not a sign”, so resting hands are ignored. It is never offered as an answer.

Reference videos: signs have a clip from the SLT community PSL dictionary (CC BY 4.0, one performer, not yet checked by Deaf signers; a dictionary entry may be a regional variant). signs link to their entry in the national PSL dictionary at psl.org.pk. SignMate never makes up how a sign looks: where no licensed video exists, it says so. Urdu words and Roman Urdu are translations of the English word, awaiting review by an Urdu editor.

Technology

From camera to voice, on your device

Why on the device

  • Privacy. Signing is personal, and often about health or family. The camera picture never leaves the phone, because there is no server to send it to.
  • Works offline. After one visit, or “Save for offline use”, it works with no signal: in a clinic basement, on a bus, during an outage.
  • Free to run. No server costs and no account, so nobody has to pay per translation.
  • No waiting on the network. A sign is read in milliseconds on the device (see the measurements below).

Limitations

  • Only signs, one at a time. Many are objects and animals. SignMate does not follow continuous signing or PSL grammar yet, and it cannot finger-spell names.
  • Signer variety. The training data comes from one dataset with no signer IDs. On one new signer the first answer was right times out of .
  • Dataset size. recordings in total, about 90 per sign; Come, Give and Want have only phone recordings and are not measured.
  • Lighting and framing. Dim light, a bright window behind you, or hands leaving the picture make tracking fail. The app runs a short camera check and tells you what to fix.
  • Hands only. The model reads the two hands, not the face or body, which carry meaning in PSL.
  • Not yet validated by Deaf signers. No sign has a Deaf validator's sign-off. All are Tier B (“Suggested, please confirm”) and are Tier A.
  • Not an interpreter. For medical, legal or emergency decisions, use a qualified PSL interpreter.

Plan to test with Deaf signers and PSL teachers

  1. Ask first. Contact Deaf Reach / FESF, the Pakistan Association of the Deaf and ConnectHear, plus PSL teachers at local Deaf schools. Participants are paid and give informed consent.
  2. Check every sign. A Deaf PSL teacher reviews each sign's label, Urdu word and reference video. A sign gets the “Validated by Deaf signers” badge only with a recorded sign-off (validator ID and date).
  3. Usability round 1. 5–8 Deaf PSL users and 3 hearing partners try the five core tasks; success rates and quotes go into a public report.
  4. A fair test set. At least 10 Deaf signers the model never saw, from at least 3 cities (Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad first). Hand points only by default; video only with opt-in. Frozen and never trained on.
  5. Only ship what is better. A new model replaces the current one only if it beats it on that test set, side by side.

Privacy

Your camera picture never leaves your device: SignMate works with hand points, keeps them only in memory, and saves nothing unless you choose to. There are no accounts and no analytics on this site. Two exceptions, both from your browser rather than from SignMate: while you are online, Google's MediaPipe library may send anonymous speed and usage counts (never images), and voice typing on the Reply screen uses your browser's speech service, which may send audio online. Typing does not.
