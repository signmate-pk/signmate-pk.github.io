How it works

Show. Confirm. Speak.

Three steps, and the signer stays in control: SignMate never speaks a word that nobody confirmed.

  1. 1

    Show the sign

    Sign one word in front of the camera. MediaPipe finds the hands, the face and the upper body in every frame, on your device. When your hands come down, SignMate knows the sign has ended.

  2. 2

    Confirm the word

    SignMate suggests a word in English, with the sign's gloss underneath. If it is not sure, it shows its top three and asks “Which one did you sign?”. If it cannot tell, it says so. Nothing is spoken until you tap Yes.

  3. 3

    Speak, and get a reply

    SignMate says the word aloud in English and always shows it as text too. Words build into a sentence. The hearing person types or speaks a reply, which appears in large type.

About the camera

  • Your browser will ask to use the camera. Choose Allow; SignMate cannot work without it.
  • The video stays on this device. SignMate turns each frame into landmark points in memory and never uploads, records or stores the picture. There is no server that could receive it.
  • You can stop the camera at any time with the Stop button, or by closing the tab.
  • If you would rather not use a camera, the guided tour replays recorded signs instead.

Tips for a good result

  • Face a light source, with both hands, your shoulders and your face in view, about an arm's length or more from the camera.
  • Start with your hands down. Raise them, make one sign, then lower them again.
  • Good signs to start with: hello, thank you, please, yes, no, water. The sign library lists all .

Technology

From camera to voice, on your device

Why on the device

  • Privacy. Signing is personal, and often about health or family. The camera picture never leaves the device, because there is no server to send it to.
  • Free to run. No server costs and no account, so nobody has to pay per translation.
  • No waiting on the network. A sign is read in milliseconds on the device (see the measurements below).

Limitations

  • Only signs, one at a time. They are everyday words, many from a young child's first vocabulary (the list includes potty, owie and pajamas). SignMate does not follow continuous signing or ASL grammar yet, and it cannot fingerspell names yet.
  • Left- and right-handed signers. Left-dominant signers were read right of the time, right-dominant : a gap over our 3-point target. Averaging each sign with its mirror image is being measured as a fix.
  • Two-handed signs. The test set holds only signs with both hands in view most of the time, too few to judge.
  • Lighting and framing. Dim light, a bright window behind you, or hands leaving the picture make tracking fail. The translator runs a short camera check and tells you what to fix.
  • Not yet validated by Deaf reviewers. No sign has a recorded sign-off from a Deaf ASL user yet; every sign says so.
  • English only for now. Urdu captions come back once an Urdu editor has reviewed them.
  • Not an interpreter. For medical, legal or emergency decisions, use a qualified ASL interpreter.

Why ASL

SignMate began with Pakistan Sign Language. For ASL, large collections of signs recorded with the consent of Deaf signers exist under licences that allow this use; for PSL they do not yet, and the PSL version was right first time on only about a third of one new signer's signs. The PSL version is archived in the repository, not forgotten.

Plan to test with Deaf ASL signers

  1. Ask first. Contact Deaf organisations and ASL teachers; participants (remote is fine) are paid and give informed consent.
  2. Check every sign. A sign gets the “Validated by Deaf signers” badge only with a recorded sign-off from a Deaf ASL user (reviewer ID and date).
  3. A real-world test set. At least 5 consenting Deaf ASL signers on a laptop and a phone, landmarks only. Frozen and never trained on.
  4. Only ship what is better. A new model replaces the current one only if it beats it on the same test sets, side by side.

What comes next

Aims, not claims: each step ships only when its measurement passes.

  1. Next Per-sign results and a fix for the left/right gap, measured on the rerun set; outreach to Deaf ASL reviewers.
  2. Then Live captions while signing, and fingerspelling of names letter by letter.
  3. Then A vocabulary of 1,000 and then 2,000 signs, measured on signers the model never saw.
  4. Later Type → Sign: typed English shown as real, licensed ASL sign recordings, word by word.

Privacy

Your camera picture never leaves your device: SignMate works with landmark points, keeps them only in memory, and saves nothing unless you choose to. There are no accounts and no analytics on this site. Two exceptions, both from your browser rather than from SignMate: while you are online, Google's MediaPipe library may send anonymous speed and usage counts (never images), and voice typing on the Reply screen uses your browser's speech service, which may send audio online. Typing does not.
